# Curve Fever Pro Tournament Hub — Future Projects Roadmap

This file contains future projects and architectural questions not yet scoped or built. An entry moves to `HANDOFF_LOG.md` once built (with the current-state docs updated), then is removed from this file. Smaller, shorter-horizon gaps and decisions are in `docs/open-items.md` instead; what is built next, and in what order, is its "Work queue", and which entries are worth building together is its "Build bundles".

The sections follow the same topics as `docs/open-items.md`: schedule structure, formats, Custom bracket, scoring, seeding, roster and organiser tools, tournaments and accounts, viewer features, and work outside the app.

## Schedule structure and room sizes

- **How rigid the bracket structure is: who decides "top 4 of 8 go through"** — raised by the organiser 2026-10-30, querying the claim "Semi-finals: in the app's structure, the top 4 of 8 go through". The organiser expects the app to make judgment calls on what a field needs, not apply one fixed split. To check first: where the claim comes from (the Info tab's rules text, a viewer explanation, or the community), and what is actually fixed today: the Final at the format's room size (FFA 8), so 4 per Semis room in two rooms (`docs/rules.md`, "single-elimination", Semis size), against what already adapts (Semis rooms shrinking to the units advancing, the "Semis/Final headcount override", lucky losers, the fair cut). Then a review of the app's flexibility across bracket formats, and a plan where a fixed number should become a derived one. Part of the same review (organiser's list of early November 2026): **the "Semis" as a special stage** may no longer mean much now that a Custom bracket can hold several semi-finals with their own match formats; whether single elimination keeps a named Semis stage (its own size rules, `semisGames`/`semisBestOf`, the Semis floor `getMinimumBracketUnits`) or treats it as one more elimination round is decided here. Related: "Room-size policy" below.
- **Room-size policy: flexible sizes, a preference order, and a strict lock** — raised by the organiser 2026-10-16 when the Semis was allowed to shrink to the units advancing (12–16 in FFA), extended in early November 2026 (the preference order) and on 2026-11-04 (the strict lock). Two sides of one setting:
  - *Flexible*: rooms of 6–8 stay the preferred FFA sizes, but the app should accept anything from 4 to 10 when a count needs it, as a last resort, and possibly a Final other than 8. Not prioritised in automatic scheduling, but possible at least in a Custom bracket when entered by hand. The organiser's preference order: **8 > 7 > 6 > 9 = 5 > 10 = 4**. Care needed that it doesn't weaken room creation for the counts that work today.
  - *Strict*: sometimes the original format must not be touched at all: no room of 7 in an 8-player FFA, no 2v2v2 room in a 2v2v2v2 tournament, even where it would help the elimination numbers. A lock that tells the app to plan everything strictly for the sizes and counts given, and to refuse (or ask) when a count can't be met that way.
  
  Both are one rule seen from either end: the set of room sizes the app may use, and in what order it prefers them. Touches `roomSize` in `formats.ts`, `distributeRooms` (`room-distribution.ts`), the Semis floor (`getMinimumBracketUnits`, `schedule-generation.ts`), team formats' teams per room, and every place that reads `roomSize.min`/`max` as a hard limit (live line-up edits, reserve over-cap confirm, Kings Valley's size limit). Best planned after the rigidity review above, which lists where sizes are derived.
- **Showing the app's decisions next to the overrides** — raised by the organiser 2026-11-04. Setup should say what the app has decided for the current count and settings, in a way the organiser can compare with the override fields at a glance: "2 qualification rounds (override: 3)", "4 rounds after the qualification", "Semis: 2 rooms of 6", "lucky losers: 2". The rigidity review above produces the list of derived numbers; this is its display. Today the Setup schedule preview (`SchedulePreviewPanel`, `schedule-preview-rows.ts`) shows the drawn rounds, and the overrides are separate fields.
- **Same rules everywhere: a consistency audit of points and elimination methods** — raised by the organiser 2026-10-30. Points systems, cuts, lucky losers, tie handling and the like should work the same across every format and phase unless there is a good reason. Known differences to start from: the fair cut is not in Kings Valley or a Custom bracket (`docs/open-items.md`, "Kings Valley and waterfall rounds are not covered by the fair cut"); group stages rank by points per match while Swiss and qualification tables use total points; match results exist only in head-to-head pooling. The audit lists each difference, keeps the justified ones (written into `docs/rules.md`) and plans the others away. Natural companion of the rigidity review: both walk the whole schedule engine.
- **Letting the app choose the best setup for a field** — two raisings merged. (1) 2026-09-21: v1 of the waterfall/rank-band bracket is fully organiser-authored (a `ROUNDS:`/`ROUTES:` text mini-language, generation-time only); a later project could have the app derive such a structure from the field size, or judge whether a waterfall-style format fits a given field at all. The mini-language's parsed graph is the natural target representation for any generator (see "Waterfall / rank-band bracket phase" in `HANDOFF_LOG.md`). (2) 2026-11-04: for any given situation the app suggests the setup it considers best, e.g. an FFA tournament of 32: with a qualification table or not, how many qualify for the Final outside the semi-finals, and so on. (3) 2026-11-10: "Advance to bracket" defaults to 24 in every format, and the upper and lower bracket's automatic entry is to follow it too (organiser's decision for now); 24 can be spot on or far off depending on the game mode and the head count, so this default is one of the numbers the app has to learn to work out. Needs the evidence of the next entry first, and the derived numbers of the rigidity review.
- **Which pooling system ranks best, for a given mode and player count** — raised by the organiser 2026-10-25 after the room-size fairness simulations. Scoring adjustments barely move accuracy, but the format itself might. Simulated with a qualification table of 4 rounds (round 1 not counted) and today's draw, the truly strongest units that qualify are:
  - 2v2v2v2, top 16: 13.1 of 16 at 25 teams, down to 12.3 at 31, 11.6 at 37 and 10.4 at 53;
  - FFA 37 and 43: about 5.6–5.8 of the true top 8 and 12.8–13.1 of the true top 16.

  The units that miss out are nearly always just below the cut, so this is the noise of a few counted rounds, not the scoring (log entries "Room-size fairness simulation (report only)" and "FFA points tables with a cut near the top (simulation, report only)"). The project would compare pooling systems on the same footing, the share of the true top k that reaches the bracket (or an equivalent where a system has no table):
  - more counted rounds, or round 1 counted;
  - Swiss, group stage;
  - a waterfall with second chances, double elimination, Kings Valley.

  It would compare them per mode (predictability differs a lot between FFA and team modes, `docs/real-scores.md`) and per player count, against the time each system takes (rounds played). The dev-only simulation (`scripts/seeding-simulation/`) already has the measures, real-score calibration and awkward counts; new systems would need runners. It is the evidence side of "Letting the app choose the best setup" above.

## Formats

- **A flexible upper and lower bracket: any number of rooms on each side** — raised by the organiser 2026-11-10 after the production test of tournament `1794254969126`. The built-in format has two fixed shapes, two rooms (one upper, one lower) or four (two and two), so only some head counts can be seated (FFA 12–16 and 24–32, head-to-head 4 or 8) and the rest are cut down or refused (`docs/open-items.md`, "An upper and lower bracket takes 16 at 17–23 in FFA and 32 above 32"). That limit was an assumption of the first build, never a decision of the organiser: nothing in the format requires it. The project: upper and lower each take as many rooms as the field needs, not necessarily the same number on both sides (two upper rooms and one lower, or the opposite), and more than two per side with many units or small rooms; the later rounds then funnel those rooms into the Semis and the Final. Open questions for its plan: how the app picks the number of rooms on each side (and the organiser's override), how unequal sides merge round by round, where ranking rooms fit, and how it meets "Room-size policy" and "How rigid the bracket structure is" (it is another case of the app deriving a structure from the head count). A further idea, not for this project: more than two levels (upper, middle, lower), which comes close to Kings Valley.

- **Swiss as a system for the whole tournament** — raised by the organiser 2026-11-04. Today Swiss is only a pooling phase in front of a bracket, head to head only (`swiss`, 3–7 fold-paired rounds, `docs/rules.md`; `swissFoldPair`, `docs/seeding.md`). The project: a tournament that is Swiss from start to finish, its final standings being the result, with the tie-breaks and pairing rules such a format needs. Two parts the organiser named, to check first for how standard they are:
  - **Buchholz** (the sum of the opponents' scores) as a tie-break. In chess it is the usual first Swiss tie-break (FIDE's tie-break rules, with variants that drop the lowest or highest opponent, "Buchholz Cut 1" / "Median Buchholz"); today the app's tie-break is the score relative to the room average.
  - **Never meeting the same opponent twice**. An absolute rule in FIDE's Swiss pairing systems as long as a pairing exists; today `swissFoldPair` only swaps to avoid a rematch when one swap clears both pairs, so a rematch can happen. Related: "Counting how many times a pair met" (Seeding and draws).
  
  To decide: whether Swiss stays head to head or extends to multi-unit rooms (an FFA Swiss has no standard pairing rule, and its "opponents" for Buchholz are a whole room), how many rounds a given count needs (the usual guide is about log2 of the field), and whether a short bracket may still follow.
- **Double elimination after a pooling phase: entering the Winners or the Losers bracket by place** — raised by the organiser with test tournament `1793550672870` (12 teams of 4v4, group stage of 3 groups of 4, 8 through into the built-in double elimination). Every qualifier enters the Winners bracket today; the organiser wants the pooling results to split them, the top places into the Winners bracket and the lower qualifying places straight into the Losers bracket (a Custom bracket can already route by Qual or Groups rank; the built-in double elimination cannot). The same tournament's double-elimination columns were also found poorly organised (no detail recorded yet: to look at with the organiser when this is planned). Touches the double-elimination generation (`double-elimination.ts`) and the pooling exit (`seedFromPoolingExit`). The entry by pooling place for an upper and lower bracket is built in `upper-lower.ts` (a Custom bracket the app derives); this entry does not reuse it yet.
- **Kings Valley early-exit / skip-ahead** — the part of the original item 7 that the waterfall/rank-band project (2026-09-21) explicitly did *not* cover: `kingsValleyBracketPhase`/`kingsValleyComputeAdvancement` run an unconditional promote/stay/demote/eliminate cycle every round with no early-exit branch, and share no primitive with the `winnersTo`/`pendingBracketSeeds` machinery the waterfall bracket reuses. Raised by the organiser (2026-10-01) as worth planning: cumulative with the ladder, e.g. the top room's winner qualifies straight for the Final in the same round the bottom room loses two. Kept separate from the Kings Valley room-sizing fix of the same date; that fix's per-room move solver (`kings-valley.ts`) is where a "straight to the Final" outflow band would plug in, as a second out-of-ladder outflow beside elimination.
- **A group exit cut that compares teams across groups, whatever their place** — raised by the organiser 2026-11-01, after the fair cut of tournament `1793467828364` (groups of 5, 4, 4, 10 through). Both total exits (`even-split`, `fair-cut`, `group-exit.ts`) go by place today: the top places of every group go through directly, and only the next place is compared across groups. With equal groups, two groups therefore never lose more than one team apart: from 4/4/4 with 3 eliminations, every group loses its 4th, so a weak group's 3rd stays while a strong group's 4th with more points per match goes. The idea is a cut where only each group's winner is safe and the rest go through on points per match, so a weak group can lose two while a strong one loses none, even with equal groups. Not yet known to be desirable: a group's results don't necessarily reflect skill, since of two weak teams in one group one will likely beat the other and look stronger than it is. To decide: whether the results are worth it, what keeps a group's results comparable across groups, and its name beside "Fair cut".

## Custom bracket (waterfall)

- **Uneven win targets in one match** — raised by the organiser 2026-10-30, not needed for the first Best-of build. A custom bracket can bring two units into a match where one must win e.g. 2 games and the other 3, as the double-elimination race Grand Final already does (`grandFinalWbTarget` / `grandFinalLbTarget`, `finals.ts`). To decide: a head start in wins (one side starts 1–0 in a first to 3) or a target per entrant, how the graph or the editor names it (per feeding source), and whether the race Grand Final then moves onto the same mechanism.
- **Band boundaries in a waterfall graph carry very different stakes** — seen in tournament `1792339397443` (2026-10-22): its graph gave the top 8 of the qualification table a second life and ranks 9–16 a single one, so with equal strengths a top-8 team reached the Final about 7 times as often (about 44% against 6%), and 8th against 9th was decided by a tie-break difference of 0.00018 among four teams on 10 points. Inside a band the qualifying rank barely matters (#1 and #8 are drawn alike; see "rank-order seating inside a destination" in `docs/open-items.md`). Ideas, none requested: a playoff room for units tied at a band boundary, the graph editor showing each band's path to the Final, or advice to put boundaries where the standings are less often tied (more counted rounds).

## Scoring, ties and match formats

- **Tie wins and tie losses in every format** — raised by the organiser 2026-10-30. Match results (Win / Tie Win / Tie Loss / Loss / Tie by the gap against a tie margin, `match-results.ts`) exist for a Group Stage and for Swiss or a qualification table of head-to-head rooms. The organiser wants the idea integrated format by format, with two decisions each time: **the window** for a tie win or loss (the margin), and **how it shows in the points**. Open: whether it means anything in multi-unit rooms (FFA, 2v2v2v2: a near-tie between two places), in eliminations (where only the cut matters) and in Best-of matches. The two entries below are the head-to-head parts already raised.
- **Match results for Best of 5 and 7** — raised 2026-10-31. Only a Best of 3 is mapped (2–0 a Win, 2–1 a Tie Win); generation refuses a Best of 5 or 7 group round under match results. To decide: which game scores (3–0, 3–1, 3–2 …) are a Win and which a Tie Win.
- **Tie-margin defaults per mode, target score and in-game scoring type** — raised 2026-10-31. The game has three target scores and several in-game scoring types (flat, flattened, percent), and the margin that suits a tie differs by mode (Save your Buddy and the others apart). Today `defaultTieMargin` (`match-results.ts`) holds one value, `team-4v4: 100` at target 1400, any other format starts with an empty field, and the margin is not adjusted for a team a player short. The defaults need the target score to be a value the app knows (today it is only free text in round notes), and Setup has to show and edit them. The organiser is gathering the values.
- **Counting only each unit's best games of a round** — raised by the organiser 2026-10-30, not needed for the next tour. In a round of several games (e.g. a Final of 3), only each unit's best x scores count (e.g. the best 2 of 3), chosen per unit: a unit with a bad game 3 drops game 3, another with a bad game 1 drops game 1. Fits beside the existing per-round match formats (`docs/rules.md`, "Match formats": "N games, summed" or "Best of N"), as a third kind, "best x of N, summed", applied by the same `applyMatchFormats` and read wherever a summed round's total is (`scoring.ts`, `comparableScore`, the Final's ranking, the room's game-score columns showing which games were dropped). To decide: whether it applies to every stage or only the Final, and how an absent game counts.

## Seeding and draws

- **Grading units into levels for the draw** — raised by the organiser twice: for the 4v4 tour (rank teams in value groups, so that a qualifying room or group holds one team of group A, one of group B, and so on) and on 2026-11-04 in general: grade players or teams into levels (1st, 2nd, 3rd) that the draw then uses: a group of 2 from level 1, 2 from level 2 and 2 from level 3, or a draft tour (teams formed by picking from each level). Today the first round is a random shuffle (Fisher-Yates) and later draws use results only (`docs/seeding.md`). Needs a level per unit in the roster (Setup and the Players tab), a draw that spreads levels over rooms or groups (the tier mechanism `tieredSeed` already spreads place tiers this way), and a decision on what levels do after round 1 (nothing, or a tie-break of the draw). Other uses the organiser mentioned ("and other things") are open.
- **Seeding qualification rounds by results** — raised by the organiser after the audit of tournament `1792339397443` (2026-10-22): the tiered draw (`tieredSeed`) seats one unit of each place tier per room, so a room winner meets weaker opponents next round. Kept as it is for now; the organiser wants to explore it further and take it up with the community. A simulation on the real engine (`scripts/seeding-simulation/`) compared draw policies, and in a second part rooms built around the cuts; its figures are in the log entry "Smaller-room seats spread evenly, places-only placeholders, the "Expected" draw, and a seeding simulation" (2026-10-23). In short: the current draw is the most accurate but has strong units meet weaker ones and leaves about 4 units tied for 1st; drawing the last round by standings keeps the accuracy with fewer ties; counting every round, round 1 included, is the most accurate setup.
- **Counting how many times a pair met** — `state.roomHistory` keeps only the last round a pair shared (`recordRoomHistory`), so a pair that met twice weighs the same as one that met once in the repeat cost. It doesn't matter in short events; in long FFA or Swiss pooling a third meeting isn't penalised more than a second. Changing it means a count beside the last round, and the rewind (`rewindRoomHistory`) rebuilding both. Needed by a strict no-rematch rule ("Swiss as a system for the whole tournament").
- **Searching every wave combination together in small waterfall and double-elimination draws** — raised in the audit of tournament `1792339397443` (2026-10-22). `tieredBracketSeed` (waterfall and double-elimination rounds) places one wave at a time (one unit per room, in rank order) and never revisits an earlier wave's choice. Scoring every combination of wave choices together, with the same cost (recency-weighted repeats, room balance) and the same one-unit-per-room-per-wave rule, finds draws the wave-by-wave fill misses: that tournament's Round 5-1 could have had 2 repeat pairings instead of 3. Combinations stay small for small rounds (two rooms of 4: 8; three rooms of 4: 1,296; two FFA rooms of 8: 256); above a size limit today's fill would stay. Low impact (one repeat saved there, from a round that didn't count); the organiser asked for the details before deciding. Note: that tournament's Round 5-2, which reunited the three teams of one qualifying room, was already the best draw that keeps each rank pair (9/10, 11/12, …) in different rooms; avoiding it would mean giving up that spread.
- **Review the audit observations of tournament `1792339397443`** — three points from the audit that are not projects yet but need a look at some point: shared points deciding a qualifying cut, a late joiner seeded slightly below the room average, and lower-band units playing more rounds to reach the Final. Details in the log entry "Audit of tournament 1792339397443: observations kept for review".

## Roster and organiser tools

- **One place for the roster: the Players tab** — raised by the organiser 2026-11-04. The roster is loaded in Admin (Setup's text boxes, "Load roster & reserves") but listed and managed in Players / Rankings (add, rename, swap, remove, reserves, confirmations, `RankingsRoster.tsx`), which is messy. The organiser leans towards moving everything into the Players tab. Raised with it, and best designed together:
  - the tab's name for admins: "Rankings" holds most of the player administration once the tournament starts, so an admin may need another name (one name for everyone is less confusing; to decide);
  - a better way to change a team's line-up than today's ⇄ prompts (`window.prompt` menus), e.g. drag and drop;
  - the Setup roster reload, which resets earlier pre-start swaps and removals (`docs/open-items.md`, "Reloading the roster in Setup resets it to the text boxes").
- **Line-up corrections in every round, a swap between rooms included** — raised by the organiser: swapping two players between rooms should stay possible after the pooling phase ("you never know if it might be necessary"). Today line-up edits are refused in scored rounds, fixed-draw pooling rounds, and in group stage (beyond the group swap), double elimination, Kings Valley and Custom bracket rounds (`docs/rules.md`, "Line-up corrections"; `docs/open-items.md`, "Live corrections have gaps by design"). The project extends the swap of two unscored units to every round in play, with the replay checks a Custom bracket's live graph edit already has.
- **Undoing an organiser decision** — raised by the organiser: a tie-break pick, an absentee answer, a no-show and the like can't be taken back after a wrong click. Today the way back is indirect: retyping the score clears an absentee answer (`setRoundScore`) and a tie pick when the tie changes (`invalidateStaleTieResolutions`); a removal has no undo. The project: a visible "change" on each recorded decision (tie picks, shared points, absentee answers, removals while nothing depends on them), and how far back each one can go. Related: "no edit history or undo" in `docs/open-items.md`, "Live corrections have gaps by design".
- **Late joiners in a group stage** — raised by the organiser 2026-10-16 with the late-joiner build. Adding a reserve is refused in a group stage because membership and the round-robin schedule are built at generation. Taking one needs the groups rebuilt mid-stage (a joiner into the smallest group, the future fixtures regenerated, the standings kept). Touches `pooling.ts`, `addReserveUnit` and the group-stage rounds. With per-match group standings a joiner starts with no counted matches (matches missed are not counted against it, `docs/rules.md`, "Group stage standings"), so the standings would need no make-up rule; whether a joiner can still qualify with few matches is for the organiser.
- **Late joiners in a fixed draw** — raised 2026-10-16. A fixed draw publishes every pooling round at generation, so a joiner has no seat. Taking one means patching the published future rounds (`patchFutureFixedDrawRounds`, `fixed-draws.ts`, as a removal or swap already does) so the joiner is paired in each of them without breaking the room-history guarantees the draw was built for.
- **A waterfall without a pooling phase taking a joiner** — raised 2026-10-16. Such a waterfall's graph is built for the exact entrant count. The organiser wants a joiner accepted while nobody is eliminated, with the graph adjusted by hand afterwards ("Edit graph"). Open questions: where the joiner is seated, and what the graph editor shows for a first round that is over its declared size.

## Tournaments, accounts and safety

- **"Unofficial" tournaments, and admins limited to them** — raised by the organiser 2026-11-04 (and on 2026-10-20, when a test tournament added on the live site did not show on the Tournaments page). Today a production "Test run" is hidden from viewers: not listed, and its link answers "This tournament isn't available" (`docs/views.md`, "What loads on a visit"). An "Unofficial" mark beside it would be visible and listed, but marked as not part of the official tour. Later step: some users get admin rights limited to Unofficial tournaments, which needs a role below Tour Host (CFP roles, `docs/platform.md`, "Login and permissions") and a server check per tournament (`requireTourAdminPermission`).
- **Export and import a tournament** — raised by the organiser, for safety: a whole tournament saved to a file and loaded back in the same format. The legacy app had an archive import (log 2026-09-06, "Archive import + minimal archive delete"); the rebuild has none (`downloadJson` in `src/lib/browser-download.ts` is unused). To decide: what an import does to an existing id (a copy under a new id is safest), who may import (admins only, server-validated like every write), and whether an editable text view of a Custom bracket graph (removed 2026-10-27) comes back as part of it.
- **How CFP handles login tokens, and silent session renewal** — raised by the organiser 2026-10-24 with the expired-session build (log: "Expired CFP session: one clear message, a sign-in dialog, nothing typed is lost"), after tournament `1792830379312` could not be published or started on test ("Invalid or expired token"). To check on CFP's side, not in this repo: how long an access token lives (the Hub's cookie lasts 30 days, `SESSION_MAX_AGE_SECONDS`), whether a new login for the same account elsewhere (the game, the production site, another device) invalidates the older tokens, whether the test API (`api-test`) and production issue separate tokens, and what a refresh looks like. `/auth/logintoken` already returns a fresh `accessToken`, which `AuthProvider.restoreSession` stores on a page load but the server-side check (`requireTourAdminPermission`) never does. If tokens turn out to be short-lived, the project is a silent renewal: refresh the token before it ends, on the client and on the server's cache, so a long tournament night never reaches the sign-in dialog. Until then the expired-session build only reports the end of a session and keeps the organiser's unsent edits.
- **Players confirm their own participation, and the schedule follows confirmations** — raised by the organiser 2026-10-18 with the confirmation marks, extended later: the marks help the organisation but don't yet decide who is in the schedule. Today only an admin marks a player (`state.confirmations`, by name; a score also confirms), and nothing in generation reads confirmations. Two parts: (1) the schedule includes only confirmed units (in a team format, to decide: every member confirmed, or enough to play), with "generate with confirmed players only" and a "remove unconfirmed" button; (2) a player confirms themselves, which needs a way to tie a signed-in account to a roster name, a write path that is not admin-only, and a decision on what an unconfirmed player at the deadline means (reserves, a smaller format). Part 1 stands alone and comes first.

## Viewer features

- **A draft schedule while "Starting soon"** — raised by the organiser. Before Confirm & Start (about 15 minutes before the start) viewers see no draw (log 2026-10-17, "A viewer's Players tab doesn't show the draw before the start"), since rooms can still change. A draft could show what the schedule looks like (rounds, room counts and sizes, formats, who advances) without saying who starts in which room. The Bracket already projects future rounds as placeholders ("Round 3 · 2nd"); the draft would show Round 1 the same way.
- **The Final as a live leaderboard** — raised by the organiser. In a Final of several games, the Final box could reorder after each game to show the current standing, which probably needs a "next game" step for the organiser (today the game scores fill in and the order is the room's result once complete).
- **Round notes linked to CFP room settings** — raised by the organiser 2026-10-25 with the round settings notes build. A note could one day be built from the actual settings of a CFP room instead of free text, if CFP ever exposes them; free text for now. Check first whether the target score is the setting that changes most often between rounds.
- **"What do I need?" line for a followed player during pooling** — raised during the Standings rethink (2026-10-05) and parked by the organiser: during qualification/Swiss/group-stage rounds, tell a followed player what result in their current room would keep them inside the qualifying cut (e.g. "needs to finish top 2 in Room C to stay safe"). Speculative by nature: the answer depends on every other room's results, so it would need a clear definition first (guaranteed vs likely, worst case over the other rooms) and wording that never promises more than the standings can know. Natural home: Bracket's follow banner, beside the standings rank.

## Outside the app (tour organisation)

- **Waiting for rooms between rounds** — noted by the organiser: the next round's rooms can't be anticipated (any algorithm drawing match-ups from results makes that unavoidable), so players wait until someone creates the CFP room, several create one at the same time, and lobbies get merged. A help would be rooms pre-created in the game, one per Bracket room, ready to join. Mostly on CFP's side; the app's part, if any, would be naming each room in a way players can find in the game, or a link per room if CFP ever offers one.
