# Room seeding

How units land in rooms from round to round (`src/domain/tournament/seeding.ts`, plus `fixed-draws.ts`). Read when a task touches transitions between rounds, reseeding, or Bracket's future-round projections, or when explaining "how did this player end up in this room". Build history and reasoning: search `HANDOFF_LOG.md` for "Diversity + rematch-aware room reseeding" and the entries named below.

## Which mechanism runs where
| Mechanism | Used for |
|---|---|
| `randomSeed` | Round 1 only: a shuffle via the injectable `RandomSource`. |
| `swissFoldPair` | Adaptive Swiss rounds: fold-pairs by rank (1 vs `half+1`, …), with a rematch-avoidance swap when one clears both pairs. |
| `tieredSeed` | Every ordinary room-based transition: no-elim warm-up, qual-table, single-elimination and team-format cuts, up to the round that seeds Semis; not the first round after a pooling exit (`snakeSeed` below), where it only fills a `diversity` or `balance` override. |
| `tieredBracketSeed` | Double elimination's winners/losers-bracket routing (race and shared-Final variants). |
| `snakeSeed` | The first bracket round after a qualification table, Swiss or group stage (and a Custom bracket round fed only by the Qual or the Groups line), through `seedFromPoolingExit` / `seedPoolByStanding`: the units in exit order (best first) go into the rooms 1..R, then R..1, then 1..R again, a full room skipped, so with 8 into 4 matches it is 1v8, 2v7, 3v6, 4v5 and with 16 into rooms of 4 `1,8,9,16 \| 2,7,10,15 \| 3,6,11,14 \| 4,5,12,13`. Room sizes are respected (23 into [8, 8, 7]: the 22nd and 23rd take the free seats of rooms 2 and 1). |
| `avoidMetPairsNearRank` | The separation after the snake for a qualification table or Swiss exit: for a pair that has met before (any earlier shared room), the lower-ranked unit trades with the unit one rank below it in the exit order, then the one above, then the same for the higher-ranked unit; a trade only between two rooms and only when it strictly lowers the met pairs of the two rooms; a unit trades at most once, so none moves more than one rank; one greedy pass. After groups the separation is `avoidSameGroupInFirstBracketRound` instead, also for a Custom bracket round fed only by the Groups line. |
| `sequentialSeed` | Kings Valley's ladder moves, including the first hop into Kings Valley: slices an ordered list into declared room sizes, no reordering. |
| `avoidSameGroupInFirstBracketRound` | The first bracket round after a group stage, applied to what `tieredSeed` or `sequentialSeed` drew: a unit that shares a room with a group-mate swaps with a unit of another room that fits (its group not in this room, the mover's group not in that room). The partner is preferably a unit of the **same group place** (place in the group's exit table; a chance or refill pick counts at the place it took), the closest to the mover in exit order and then the first room, so a winner stays against a runner-up rather than meeting another winner; with none of that place that fits, the first unit that fits. Best effort: a collision with no fitting swap stays, byes are never moved. |
| Fixed draws | Qual-table/Swiss with draw publication `fixed`: the whole pooling schedule is computed at generation. |
| `distributeTierMajorPool` | Bracket's future-round **display** projection only (`projectFutureRoundSlots`). |

## `tieredSeed`
Survivors are grouped into rank tiers across all rooms (tier 0 = every room's winner, tier 1 = every runner-up, …). Inside a tier the units are ordered by their score relative to their room's average (`pct`, `relativeRoomShare`: score ÷ room total × units in the room, 1 = average), then by source room, then by name. It is relative, the same measure that ranks lucky losers, because rooms differ in size (an average unit takes 1/3 of a room of three but 1/4 of a room of four, so a raw share would favour small rooms); with rooms all of one size it orders exactly as the raw share does. A bye (tier 0) takes the highest relative score the round allows, its largest room size, so it sorts first; a late joiner is an average unit, 1. `pct` only orders units inside a tier and is never compared across tiers. The next round's rooms are filled one wave (a tier, or part of one) at a time; each wave is assigned to rooms by minimising a weighted cost of:
- repeat pairings against everyone already placed and all prior rounds, recency-weighted (a pair that met last round costs ~10% more than one that met long ago), and
- how far each room's running total lands from balanced.

A room's tiers come from `buildAdvancementTiers`: a unit's tier is its place in its room, and units that shared a place (shared points, `docs/rules.md`, "Scoring") share that place's tier, in every pooling round. Except at a pooling exit (the first round after the last qualification or Swiss round, or the last group round, `isStandingsCutoffRound`): the standings decide that draw, not one room's result, so every unit is its own tier in the exit order (`exitOrderTiers`) and, without a seeding override, the round is the snake above and not the wave fill. A `diversity` or `balance` override keeps the wave fill over those tiers, `random` stays random.

The weighting tapers from diversity-heavy (warm-up and early cuts) to balance-heavy by the round that seeds Semis. Match history is kept in `state.roomHistory` (pair key → last round index shared), also written by double elimination. A re-draw ("Next Round" after "Previous") first rewinds the discarded draws' entries, and entries at or after the round being drawn are ignored, so a stale or planned entry is never treated as a past repeat.

**Smaller-room seats** (`smaller-room-seats.ts`). Under the earlier points rule (a tournament without `gamemodeConfig.pointsVersion`) the same place earns the same points in any room size, so a seat in a room smaller than the round's largest is worth about half a point a round; scaled points (`pointsVersion` 2, `docs/rules.md` "Scoring") remove that worth, and the spread still evens out who plays where in both. In every room round with rooms of different sizes (`spreadsSmallerRoomSeats`: a warm-up, a qualification or Swiss round counted or not, an ordinary elimination round), `tieredSeed` therefore fills the smaller rooms apart from the larger ones, and picks who takes the smaller-room seats (`smallerRoomSeatsBefore`: derived from the line-ups of the earlier rounds that spread, never stored, so a re-draw or a removal needs no rewind; "smaller" is read from the line-up as drawn, a bye is no seat; the units absent from a room (`docs/rules.md`, "Absentees") hold no seat, and the units present in a room with absentees count one, since the room played smaller than the largest room of its line-up). A seat counts 1 in a round that earns points (a counted qualification or Swiss round, an elimination round) and 0.5 in a round that earns none (a warm-up, a round excluded from the standings, `smallerRoomSeatWeight`): such a round still eases a low-ranked unit's entry, but with a single such round it only breaks ties between units level on the rest (two such seats weigh as much as one counted seat). The two groups are then filled by the same wave logic, each over its own rooms (`fillRoomsByWaves`; the balance mean is per group). A late joiner (below) comes last for smaller-room seats in the round they enter and gets one only if every other unit already has one; from the next round their real count applies. Rematch avoidance and room balance work inside the split. A two-seat smaller room (3v3v3) can come out as two units of one tier: the balance term allows "1 + 1" next to "0 + 2", accepted at review. Semis, the Final, group stage, waterfall, double elimination and Kings Valley keep their own draw.

**Likely absentees** (`placeLikelyAbsentees`; `absenceVersion` 3, `docs/rules.md` "Absentees"). Before any wave is filled, the members of the pool that are likely absent (`likelyAbsentees(state, targetRoundIndex)`) are placed first, best tier first, one by one: each goes to a room with the fewest likely absentees so far, of those the room with the most seats left (its planned size less the absentees placed: never two in a room while another has fewer, none in a smaller room while a larger one has none), then the room the repeat pairs and balance of the draw's weights prefer, then the lowest room number. The rest of the pool is drawn exactly as above into the seats left (`seatsLeft`, the planned sizes less the absentees placed), so the smaller-room spread reads a room holding an absentee as the smaller room it is expected to be: the units that already held a smaller room's seat get the rooms expected full. The 'random' seeding override places them the same way, then shuffles the others. The result keeps its shape (`RoundAssignment[]` in pool order). Rooms of 2 (every planned size at most 2) place nobody. Worked cases (`likely-absentee-draw.test.ts`): 15 teams in 4, 4, 4, 3 with two absentees put them in two different rooms of 4; 31 (8, 8, 8, 7) with six absentees gives 2, 2, 1, 1 with one in the room of 7; 37 (8, 8, 7, 7, 7) with three fills both 8s, then a 7; 43 and 53 with one absentee more than rooms put one in every room and the extra one in a room with seats to spare.

Who takes the seats depends on whether anyone has been eliminated (`isFullFieldRound`, read from the stored rounds). **While the whole field is in** (every pooling round; then every round for which each earlier round advances all it seats, `advTotal >= players`, and no qualification table, Swiss or group stage before it has cut the pool at its exit, the cut being read from the next round's `players` falling below the exit round's; a warm-up advances everyone and a withdrawal's refit keeps that, so a withdrawal never flips it), `splitForSmallerRooms` gives the seats to the units with the fewest so far (weighted), and among the units tied at the boundary count the picks are strided over the seeding order so the smaller rooms keep a mix of tiers (best first would keep sending the top units into smaller rooms in the rounds where the spread matters most). Seat counts end within 1 of each other whenever the shape allows. With pooling "none", rounds 1 to 3 (two warm-ups and the first elimination round) are one such stretch. **After eliminations** (the second elimination round on; the first round after a qualification table, Swiss or group stage that cut the pool is the snake over the exit order, which gives each room one unit per pass, so the smaller rooms' seats are spread over the order by construction and `splitByFinishingPlace` is not used for it) results come first: `splitByFinishingPlace` shares the seats between the finishing places in proportion to each place's size (`seatsPerTier`: the floors of `seats × tier size / units`, the leftover seats to the largest fractional parts, the better place first on equal parts), so no place is rewarded or penalised for its result. The places are the tiers `tieredSeed` already builds: every unit that finished k-th in its room, a shared place in one tier; a lucky loser keeps the tier of the place it finished in, so with two advancing per room the lucky losers form the 3rd-place tier. Inside a tier the seats go to the units with the fewest so far (late joiners last); units tied on that count take the seats best first, in the tier's order (relative score above), because a seat that goes to one of them in any case is better given to the one that earned it. Example: 10 units into rooms of [4,3,3] have 6 smaller seats; tiers of 3, 3, 3 and 1 get 2, 2, 2 and 0. The smaller-room seats of the survivors then stay within 1 or 2 of each other (the price of results first), against 1 to 3 for the plain wave fill.

Behaviour worth knowing when explaining it to players: the balance term is often an exact tie (whenever a wave's candidates share a tier, and for any two-room destination starting from zero), so the taper acts almost like pure diversity in common shapes; balance only visibly matters with 3+ destination rooms. A pure `balance` seeding override (diversity weight exactly 0) ignores repeat history entirely, so it can produce more repeat opponents than a balance-heavy blend. Details: "Seeding system explainer + seeding-behavior findings" in the log.

Units with no room in the round just played are not tiered by a result. A bye keeps tier 0 with share 1 (it counts as a win). A late joiner (`lateJoinersOf`, `late-joiners.ts`: roster units neither seated in that pooling round nor on its bye) goes to the middle tier, `floor(largest room / 2)` (rooms of 7: the 4th place; 8: the 5th; head-to-head: the losers' tier), with the mean share, `rooms / units seated` (`sourceRoom` 0); from their second round on their own result tiers them. No pooling bye choice (`selectPoolingBye`, the generic bye slice) takes a joiner unless nobody else is left.

## `tieredBracketSeed`
The same wave/cost/taper approach for double elimination, where a target round can pool units from several source rounds (a losers-bracket round mixes fresh winners-bracket drops with the previous losers-bracket round's survivors). Each contribution is tagged with its room-rank tier when pushed, so the pooled entries are directly comparable. The taper (`doubleEliminationApproachProgress`) is computed per bracket side, with the terminal round at 1. A pool that holds likely absentees (`absenceVersion` 3) places them first, one to a room, in the same way as above (`likelyAbsent` option; `seedRoundFromPendingPool` passes `likelyAbsentees(state, roundIndex)`), and the others fill the seats left by the same wave logic (`fillRoomsByWaves`). A Custom bracket round fed only by the Qual or Groups line (`seedPoolByStanding`) and the first bracket round after a Qual or Swiss exit (`seedFromPoolingExit`) do the same around the snake: the absentees first, the others snake into the seats left and the separation pass works over them alone. A group stage's exit places none.

## `swissFoldPair` and late joiners
A joiner added during a Swiss round is appended last in the fold order (in add order), so index k pairs them with index k + half, a middle-ranked unit; they never get the bye.

## Fixed draws (`fixed-draws.ts`)
Computed once from roster order, never from results. Swiss uses `circleMethodSchedule` (zero repeat pairings); qual-table uses a greedy wave fill with `assignWaveToRooms` and all tiers equal. A round with mixed room sizes, counted or not, is split as above (everyone is in a fixed pooling schedule, so always the whole-field split), with the weighted seat counts of the rounds built so far and no late joiners (a fixed draw refuses them after the start). Stored per round on `TournamentRound.fixedRoomAssignments` (not in `state.assignments`, which means "round reached"); `transitions.ts` uses it directly. `roomHistory`/`poolingByeCounts` for the whole schedule are folded in at generation, and rebuilt after a removal or swap patches the unreached rounds.

## Future-round projection (Bracket display only)
`projectFutureRoundSlots` (`bracket.ts`) labels future slots with their expected origin. Where the target round has several rooms the real draw spreads the units over them, so a slot names the source round and the place only ("Round 2 · 1st", "Round 5-1 · 2nd", "Round 5 Upper · 3rd": `roomKnown` false on `room-rank` and `waterfall-rank`); a one-room target (fixed by routing) keeps the exact origin ("Room 2A, Rank 1", "Winner of Room B", a waterfall slot names its source round by the round's own label, "Round 5 Upper · Room B, Rank 2" or, for a one-room source, "Semi A, Rank 3", "★ Lucky loser (any room)", "Dropped from WB Round 6", "Qualifier from standings (seed TBD)"). The candidate pool is built tier-major and spread across target rooms by `distributeTierMajorPool` (round-robin with a rotating start), so every projected room shows exactly its declared size and a spread of ranks and source rooms. A round fed only by the Qual or Groups line is the exception: its places are known before any score, so they are sorted by rank and placed with the real draw's snake (`snakeSeed`, best against weakest: `Groups: 1-4->` into a 2x2 round previews 1 v 4 and 2 v 3), before the separation pass, which depends on results and isn't previewed; a round also fed by a waterfall room keeps the round-robin. Accepted trade-off: the real transition (`tieredSeed`/`tieredBracketSeed`) can place units differently once the round is reached. Where the origin can't be known ahead of time the slot shows "—", and every round built on it does too. Group Stage and fixed-draw rounds show their real pairings; Kings Valley future rounds show room sizes only. Under the fair cut (`cutVersion`) a source round sends on the always-through places of each room's size (`room-rank` slots, ranks 1…`direct` of that room, tier-major as before) and the rest of its target as lucky slots, so the pool still sums to the target round's seats.

**The expected draw** (`expected-draw.ts`). `expectedNextColumn(state)` returns the draw of every round of the next column as Next Round would make it, as soon as it is determined: the tournament has started, there is a next column, nothing of it is drawn or published yet (no `assignments`, `fixedRoomAssignments` or group-stage `matches`), every contested room of the column in play has every score in (`isRoomScored`, `scoring.ts`), and `advanceTournamentRound` returns `advanced` (a pending tie or any refusal gives null). It runs the real advance on the state, which is pure, and reads the next column's rooms, line-ups and byes from the result; nothing is written. The Bracket shows those rounds as future rounds with the units' names and an "EXPECTED" tag, to admins and viewers alike (`docs/views.md`, item 5), and the real round replaces them when drawn. Because it is the real advance, the expected draw includes the separation trades exactly as Next Round makes them: group-mates kept apart after a group stage, units that have met parted after a qualification table or Swiss. Only the next column: later rounds keep the place labels above.
